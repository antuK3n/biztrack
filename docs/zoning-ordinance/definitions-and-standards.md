# Part 1 — Definitions (Article III: Definition of Terms and Rules of Construction)

## Section 1 preamble (lines 293–300)

> The definition of the terms used in this Zoning Ordinance shall carry the same meaning given to them in already approved codes and regulations, such as but not limited to the National Building Code, Water Code, Philippine Environmental Code and other Implementing Rules and Regulations promulgated by the Housing and Land Use Regulatory Board. The words, terms and phrases enumerated hereunder shall be understood to have the corresponding meaning indicated as follows:
>
> Other words and phrases used in this Ordinances are hereby defined in Appendix "A" of this Ordinance which is attached herewith and made an integral part hereof, and shall be construed as being covered by such terms.

**Count of defined terms in Article III Section 1: 89.** (A further set of 89 numbered additional definitions appears in ANNEX A, line 4348 onward — outside the requested region. The preamble calls it "Appendix 'A'"; the body labels it "ANNEX A".)

## The 89 defined terms, verbatim / near-verbatim

1. **Absolute Majority Vote** — means that the "in favor" votes represent more than 50 percent of the valid votes. This is also called the 50% + 1 vote.
2. **Accessory Use** — pertains to those that are customarily associated with the Principal Use application (such as a garage is accessory to a house).
3. **Actual Use** — refers to the purpose for which the property is principally or predominantly utilized by the person in possession of the property.
4. **AFMA** — shall refer to the Agriculture and Fisheries Modernization Act of 1997 or RA 8435.
5. **Agricultural Land Use Conversion** — per RA 6657, refers to the process of changing the use of agricultural land to non-agricultural uses.
6. **Agri-Processing Activities** — "refers to the processing of raw agricultural and fishery products into semi-processed or finished products which include materials for the manufacture of food and/or non-food products, pharmaceuticals and other industrial products." (AFMA)
7. **Allowable Uses** — uses that conform to those allowed in a specific zone.
8. **Apartment building** — a building arranged, intended or designed to be occupied by three or more families living independently of each other.
9. **Aquaculture Sub-Zone (Aq-SZ)** — an area within the Municipal Waters Zone of a city/municipality designated for "fishery operations involving all forms of raising and culturing fish and other fishery species in fresh, brackish and marine water areas" (Fisheries Code).
10. **Aquasilviculture** — an environment-friendly mangrove aquaculture system, aquasilviculture promotes the harmonious co-existence between fishery species and mangrove trees in a semi-enclosed system. In many regions, the trainees are taught pen design and construction and mud crab culture. The culture system helps in providing alternative livelihood to fisher folk while instilling in them the value of coastal protection and maintenance of the ecosystem.
11. **Base Flood Elevation** — the elevation to which floodwater is expected to reach during flood events as calculated by the regional office of the DPWH.
12. **Base Zones** — refers to the primary zoning classification of areas within the City/Municipality and that are provided with a list of allowable uses and regulations on building density and bulk, among others.
13. **Basic R-2 Zone (BR2-Z)** — An area within the City intended for medium density residential use, characterized mainly by low-rise single-attached, duplex or multi-level structures residential buildings for exclusive use as multifamily dwellings.
14. **Basic R-3 Zone (BR3-Z)** — An area within city intended for low to medium density residential. Basic R3 Zone is characterized mainly by low-rise for exclusive use as multi-family dwellings with mixed housing types.
15. **Billboards Overlay Zone (BB-OZ)** — an area in a city/municipality designated for the regulated placement of billboards.
16. **Building Height Limit (BHL)** — per the National Building Code, this is "the maximum height to be allowed for buildings/structures…and shall be generally measured from the established grade line to the topmost portion of the proposed building/structure. BHL is expressed as the number of allowable storey's/floor above established grade and/or meters above highest grade."
17. **Cemetery/Memorial Park Zone (C/MP-Z)** — an area in a city/municipality intended for the interment of the dead.
18. **Certificate of Non-Conformance** — certificate issued to Owners of non-conforming uses as provided in this Zoning Ordinance.
19. **Central Business District (CBD)** — shall refer to areas designated principally for trade, services and business purposes.
20. **Civil Reservation Sub-Zone (CR-SZ)** — an area within the Forest Zone of cities/municipalities that "refers to lands of public domain which have been proclaimed by the President of the Philippines for specific purpose such as town sites, resettlement areas, ancestral lands, etc." (NSCB)
21. **Class "AAA" Slaughterhouse/Abattoir** — those with facilities and operational procedures appropriate to slaughter livestock and fowls for sale in any market, domestic or international.
22. **Class "AA" Slaughterhouse/Abattoir** — those with facilities and operational procedures sufficiently adequate that the livestock and fowls slaughtered therein is suitable for sale in any market within the country.
23. **Class "A" Slaughterhouse/Abattoir** — those with facilities and procedures of minimum adequacy that the livestock and the fowls slaughtered therein are suitable for distribution and sale only within the city or municipality where the slaughterhouse is located.
24. **Commercial-1 Zone (C1-Z)** — a low density commercial area within a city/municipality intended for neighborhood or community scale trade, service and business activities.
25. **Commercial-2 Zone (C2-Z)** — a medium to high density commercial area within a city/municipality intended for trade, service and business activities performing complementary/supplementary functions to the CBD, general commercial zone or commercial-3 zone.
26. **Commercial-3 Zone (C3-Z)** — a high density commercial area within a city/municipality intended for regional shopping centers such as large malls and other commercial and business activities which are regional in scope or where market activities generate traffic and require utilities and services that extend beyond local boundaries and requires metropolitan level development planning and implementation. High rise hotels, sports stadium or sports complexes area also allowed in this zone.
27. **Commercial Garage** — a garage where automobiles and other motor vehicles are housed, cared for, equipped, repaired or kept for remuneration, for hire or sale.
28. **Compatible Uses** — different uses capable of existing harmoniously within a zone, e.g. residential and parks and playground uses subject to the conditions stipulated in the Zoning Ordinance.
29. **Comprehensive Land Use Plan (CLUP)** — is a technical document embodying specific proposals and strategies for guiding, regulating growth and/or development that is implemented through the Zoning Ordinance. The main components of the Comprehensive Land Use Plan in this usage are the land use plan and sectoral studies including Demography, Ecosystems Analysis (Terrestrial and Coastal), and Special Area Studies such as Climate Change Adaptation, Disaster Risk Reduction and Management, Ancestral Domain, Biodiversity, Heritage Conservation and Green Urbanism.
30. **Comprehensive Development Master Plan (CDMP)** — a unitary development plan/site plan that permits flexibility in planning/urban design, building/structure sitting, complementary of building types and land uses, usable open spaces for general public uses services and business activities and the preservation of significant land features (NBC) and may also be referred to as a Master Development Plan.
31. **Commercial Fishing Sub-Zone (CF-SZ)** — an area within the Municipal Waters Zone of a city/municipality where commercial fishing, subject to the provisions of the Fisheries Code, is allowed.
32. **Conflicting Uses** — uses or land activities with contrasting characteristics and adjacent to each other e.g. residential units adjacent to industrial plants.
33. **Conforming Use** — a use that is in accordance with the zone regulations as provided for in the Ordinance.  *(FLAGGED)*
34. **Deed Restrictions** — written agreement that imposes limitations on the use of property in order to maintain the intended character of a neighborhood.
35. **Easement** — open space imposed on any land use/activities sited along waterways, fault lines, road-rights-of-way, cemeteries/memorial parks, utilities and the like.  *(FLAGGED)*
36. **Established Grade** — the finish ground level of a proposed development which shall be determined according to the provisions of the latest edition of the National Building Code.
37. **Ecotourism** — a form of sustainable tourism within a natural and cultural heritage area where community participation, protection and management of natural resources, culture and indigenous knowledge and practices, environmental education and ethics, as well as economic benefits are fostered and pursued for the enrichment of host communities and the satisfaction of visitors." (Tourism Act and DENR AO2013-19 Guidelines on Ecotourism Planning and Management in Protected Areas)
38. **Ecotourism Overlay Zone (ETM-OZ)** — an area in a City intended for ecotourism uses. The objective for this Overlay Zone is to ensure that the dual goals of environmental conservation and tourism economic development are attained.
39. **Environmentally Constrained Areas** — areas prone to natural hazards, such as those related to weather, hydrologic, and geologic disturbances. These hazards cover those that are weather and water-related, earthquake-induced, volcanic and erosion-related.
40. **Environmentally Critical Areas (ECA)** — refer to those areas which are environmentally sensitive and are listed in Presidential Proclamation 2146 dated December 1981, as follows:
    - a. All areas declared by law as national parks, watershed a reserve, wildlife preserves and sanctuaries;
    - b. Areas set aside as aesthetic potential tourist spots;
    - c. Areas which constitute the habitat for any endangered or threatened species of indigenous Philippine wildlife (flora and fauna);
    - d. Areas of unique historic, archaeological, or scientific interests;
    - e. Areas which are traditionally occupied by cultural communities or tribes;
    - f. Areas frequently visited and/or hard-hit by natural calamities (geologic hazards, floods, typhoons, volcanic activity, etc.);
    - g. Areas with critical slopes;
    - h. Areas classified as prime agricultural lands;
    - i. Recharge areas of aquifers;
    - j. Water bodies characterized by one or any combination of the following conditions: tapped for domestic purposes; within the controlled and/or protected areas declared by appropriate authorities; and which support wildlife and fishery activities.
    - k. Mangrove areas characterized by one or any combination of the following conditions: with primary pristine and dense young growth; adjoining the mouth of major river systems; near or adjacent to traditional productive fry or fishing grounds; which act as natural buffers against shore erosion, strong winds and storm floods; and on which people are dependent on their livelihood.
    - l. Coral reef characterized by one or any combination of the following conditions: with 50% and above live coralline cover; spawning and nursery grounds of fish; and which acts as natural breakwater of coastlines.
    - Proponents of Projects within ECAs are required to submit Initial Environmental Examinations to DENR Regional Offices. They may later be required by the DENR to submit an EIS, if necessary.
41. **Environmentally Critical Projects (ECP)** — refer to those projects which have high potential for negative environmental impacts and are listed in Presidential Proclamation 2146 dated December 14, 1981, as follows:
    - a. Heavy industries: non-ferrous metal industries; iron and steel mills; petroleum and petro-chemical industries including oil and gas; and smelting plants.
    - b. Resource extractive industries: major mining and quarrying projects; forestry projects such as logging, major wood processing, introduction of fauna (exotic animals) in public/private forests, forest occupancy, extraction of mangroves and grazing; fishery projects (dikes for/and fishpond development projects)
    - c. Infrastructure projects: major dams; major power plants (fossil-fuelled, nuclear-fuelled, hydroelectric or geothermal); major reclamation projects; and major roads and bridges.
    - d. Golf course projects
    - Proponents of ECPs are required to submit an EIS to the Environmental Management Bureau (EMB) of the DENR.
42. **Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) System** — pursuant to PD 1586 of 1978, refers to the entire process of organization, administration and procedure institutionalized for the purpose of assessing the significance of the effects of physical developments on the quality of the environment. Projects that fall within the purview of the EIS System include: a. Environmentally Critical Projects; b. Projects located in Environmentally Critical Areas.
43. **Estuary** — a partially enclosed body of water along the coast where freshwater from rivers and streams meets and mixes with salt water from the ocean.
44. **Exception** — a device which grants a property owner relief from certain provisions of the Ordinance where because of the specific use would result in a particular hardship upon the owner, as distinguished from a mere inconvenience or a desire to make more money.  *(FLAGGED)*
45. **Fisheries Code** — shall refer to the Philippine Fisheries Code of 1998 (RA 8550).
46. **Fish Pond** — land-based facility enclosed with earthen or stone material to impound water for growing fish." (Fisheries Code).
47. **Flood Overlay Zone (FLD-OZ)** — an area in a city/municipality that have been identified as prone to flooding and where specific regulations are provided in order to minimize its potential negative effect to developments.
48. **Flood Protection Elevation** — the minimum elevation to which developments are required by this Ordinance to be elevated, with reference to the Base Flood Elevation, in order to be flood proofed.
49. **Floor Area Ratio or "FAR"** — is the ratio between the gross floor area of a building and the area of the lot on which it stands, determined by dividing the gross floor area of the building and the area of the lot. The gross floor area of any building should not exceed the prescribed floor area ratio (FAR) multiplied by the lot area. The FAR of any zone should be based on its capacity to support development in terms of the absolute level of density that the transportation and other utility networks can support.
50. **General Commercial Zone (GC-Z)** — an area within a city/municipality intended for trading/services/business purposes.
51. **Gross Floor Area (GFA)** — the GFA of a building is the total floor space within the perimeter of the permanent external building walls, occupied by: Office areas; Residential areas; Corridors; Lobbies; Mezzanine; Vertical penetrations, which shall mean stairs, fire escapes, elevator shafts, flues, pipe shafts, vertical ducts, and the like, and their enclosing walls; Rest rooms or toilets; Machine rooms and closets; Storage rooms and closets; Covered balconies and terraces; Interior walls and columns, and other interior features. But excluding: Covered areas used for parking and driveways, including vertical penetrations in parking floors where no residential or office units are present; Uncovered areas for AC cooling towers, overhead water tanks, roof decks, laundry areas and cages, wading or swimming pools, whirlpools or Jacuzzis, gardens, courts or plazas.
52. **Heritage Act** — shall mean the National Cultural Heritage Act of 2009 or RA 10066.
53. **Heritage Overlay Zone (HTG-OZ)** — is applied in areas with houses of ancestry and other cultural heritage of the City as declared by the National Historic Institute. The objectives are: 1.) to preserve existing historic structures; and 2.) to harmonize the design and construction of new ones with the design of these historic structures.
54. **Industrial-1 Zone (I1-Z)** — an area within cities/municipalities intended for light manufacturing or production industries that are: a. non-pollutive/non-hazardous; and b. non-pollutive/hazardous.

Maximum R3 Zone is characterized mainly by low-rise to high-rise residential buildings for exclusive use as multi-family dwellings with mixed housing types allowing commercial in scale for close proximity to most residents without endangering individuality in terms of privacy and safety and with more than the usual community ancillary uses serving also the needs of the outlying area.

## Flagged terms — the ones the ordinance actually defines

- **Non-Conforming Use** — defined (#67). Keyed to *existence prior to approval of the ZO*, and such uses "are allowed to operate subject to the conditions of this Zoning Ordinance." Paired with **Notice of Non-Conformance** (#68) and **Certificate of Non-Conformance** (#18).
- **Conforming Use** — defined (#33), one line: "a use that is in accordance with the zone regulations as provided for in the Ordinance."
- **Locational Clearance** — defined three ways (#59 LC by the Zoning Administrator/Zoning Officer; #60 LC-V and #61 LC-E, both issued by the LZBA).
- **Variance** — defined (#83). Grounds are *physical* (surrounding, shape, topography); it relieves against Building Bulk and Density Regulations, Building Design Regulations, and Performance Standards.
- **Exception** — defined (#44). Grounds are the *specific use*; the drafting is garbled ("where because of the specific use would result in a particular hardship").
- **Easement** — defined (#35).
- **Buffer** — **NOT defined in Article III.** The word appears only inside other definitions (Open Space #69: "buffer/greenbelts"; ECA #40k "natural buffers"). A buffer definition exists only operatively in **Article VII Section 11**: "A buffer shall be define as an additional open space different from the building setback."
- **Business trigger** — **Article III defines no term that triggers a zoning requirement for a business as opposed to a building.** The nearest concepts are *Actual Use* (#3), *Allowable Uses* (#7), *Accessory Use* (#2) and *Central Business District* (#19), all of which attach to land/property, not to a business entity. The actual business trigger lives outside Article III, in **Article IX Section 8 (Business Permit)** — recorded here because it is the operative rule the definitions do not supply:
  > "The Business and Licensing Division shall require a Locational Clearance for new developments.
  > Should there be any change in the activity or expansion of the area subject of the Locational Clearance, the owner/developer shall apply for a new Locational Clearance."
  And **Article IX Section 9 (Validity of Locational Clearance)**: an LC expires automatically if the use/activity/development is not commenced within one year; any change in activity or expansion of area requires a new LC.

## Section 2. Construction & Interpretation of Terms (lines 640–654)

> The words and terms employed in this Ordinance shall be interpreted and understood liberally in the generic sense unless otherwise indicated and shall as far as practicable be construed in favor of applicants seeking to comply with the provisions hereof. The listing of uses shall unless otherwise indicated be similarly construed. In addition, the following rules shall be observed:
> a) generic terms such as others, and the like, etc. shall be construed to mean as including all specific terms similar to or compatible with those enumerated;
> b) the singular includes the plural, subject to density regulations;
> c) the present tense includes the future tense;
> d) the word "person" includes both natural and juridical persons;
> e) the word "lot" includes the phrase plot parcel;
> f) the term "shall" is always mandatory; and
> g) the word "used" or "occupied" as applied to any land or building shall construed to include the words "intended" "arranged", "designed to be used" or "occupied".

# Part 2 — Performance Standards (Article VI)

## Section 1. Application of Performance Standards
Standards exist "to ensure land use and neighborhood compatibility." Proposed developments shall comply with the applicable performance standards, "which shall form part of the requirements for Locational Clearance." The standards are expressly "by the no means exhaustive or all inclusive" — the **LZBA may require other standards** when deemed necessary. Enforcement is through the Implementing Guidelines made part of the ZO.
**Numeric thresholds: none.**

## Section 2. Environmental Conservation and Protection Standards
Regulates: views, groundwater, drainage/watercourses, stormwater, erosion/sedimentation, water quality, effluents, toxic/hazardous waste, floodplains, slopes, air emissions, solid waste, industrial processes. Thirteen numbered rules:
1. Views preserved for public enjoyment, especially high-scenic-quality sites, via building orientation, height, bulk, fencing, landscaping.
2. **Deep wells not allowed** unless a Water Permit is obtained from the National Water Resources Board.
3. No alteration of natural drainage patterns or of storm water velocities/volumes/physical, chemical and biological characteristics. Streams, watercourses, wetlands, lakes or ponds shall not be altered, re-graded, developed, piped, diverted or built upon.
4. Storm water runoff controlled through appropriate storm water drainage system design.
5. Protection of rivers, streams, lakes and ponds from sedimentation and erosion damage.
6. Internal drainage designed not to increase turbidity or sediment yield or discharge harmful substances. **Water quality per DENR's latest Revised Water Usage and Classification/Ambient Water Quality Criteria** (standard incorporated by reference; no figure in text).
7. Municipal and industrial wastewater effluents shall not discharge into surface and groundwater unless scientifically proven not to deteriorate water quality. **Effluents per DENR's latest Effluent Quality Standards for Class "C" Inland Waters** (incorporated by reference).
8. Toxic/hazardous waste generators shall provide handling and treatment facilities approved by DENR.
9. Floodplains not altered, filled, or built upon without proper drainage design and consideration of inundation effects on nearby properties.
10. Adequate slope/erosion protection and soil conservation, particularly in sloping areas.
11. No emission of dust, dirt, fly ash, smoke, gas or other air polluting material harmful to health or impairing visibility. **Air quality at the point of emission per DENR's latest Air Quality Standards** (incorporated by reference).
12. Significant solid-waste generators shall provide collection and disposal systems and facilities.
13. Industrial processes/activities should not cause negative impacts; the Zoning Administrator/Zoning Officer may request descriptions of these as part of the Locational Clearance requirements.
**Explicit numeric thresholds: none — all limits are delegated to DENR standards by reference.**

## Section 3. Agricultural Land Conservation and Preservation Criteria
Agricultural lands shall not be prematurely re-classified. Re-classification requests evaluated on merits of conditions prevailing at time of application, compatibility with the CLUP, and subject to Memorandum Circular No. 54 (guidelines under Sec. 20, RA 7160). Approved applications go to **HLURB/Sangguniang Panlalawigan for review and final approval**.
**Numeric thresholds: none.**

## Section 4. Network of Green and Open Spaces
Purpose: minimise urban heat islands.
1. All residential, commercial, industrial and mixed-use subdivisions (per PD 1216, PD 953, PD 957, BP 220) must provide **tree-planted strips along internal roads**.
2. Same developments must provide landscaped tree parks, which may count toward the PD 957 / BP 220 open space requirement. These mandated open spaces are classified as "no alienable public lands, and non-buildable."
3. **Roof decks of all buildings shall be landscaped**, as applicable.
4. **Parking lots with at least 20 car parking slots** shall be:
   - a. Landscaped with suitable trees — **minimum tree height at the time of securing an Occupancy Permit: 1.80 metres** from base to crown.
   - b. **50% paved with permeable or semi-permeable materials** (grass, gravel, grass pavers and the like).

## Section 5. Site Development Standards
Intent: safe, efficient, aesthetically pleasing projects in harmony with adjacent properties and bordering streets. Items as printed (the body jumps from 4 to 6 — see anomalies):
1. Height and bulk shall not impair entry of light and ventilation, cause loss of privacy, or create nuisances, hazards or inconveniences to adjacent developments.
2. **Abutments to adjacent properties not allowed without the neighbour's prior written consent**, which the Zoning Administrator/Zoning Officer shall require prior to granting a Locational Clearance.
3. Parking capacity **per the minimum requirements of the National Building Code**; landscaped; "In no case shall parking areas/lots encroach into street rights-of-way."
4. Traffic-attracting developments (shopping malls, schools, places of worship, markets, sports stadia and the like) shall provide adequate on-site parking plus vehicular loading/unloading bays so through street traffic flow is not impeded.
6. Buffers, silencers, mufflers, enclosures and other noise-absorbing materials shall be provided to all noise and vibration-producing operations. **Noise levels per DENR's latest guidelines on the Abatement of Noise and Other Forms of Nuisance** (incorporated by reference — no dB figure given).
7. Glare and heat from any operation or activity shall not be radiated, seen or felt from any point beyond the limits of the property.
8. **Fencing along roads shall be see-through.** Side and rear fencing between adjacent lots (not facing a road) may be opaque.

## Section 6. Infrastructure Capacities
Developments "shall not cause excessive requirements at public cost for public facilities and services" and shall exhibit that their requirements for public infrastructure (roads, drainage, water supply and the like) are within the capacities of the systems serving them. The Zoning Administrator shall require:
1. **Drainage Impact Assessment Study** — required of all development proposals in flood prone areas and all major proposals likely to affect the existing drainage regime, including commercial-residential buildings or condominiums, shopping malls, public markets, schools, universities, residential and industrial, and other similar developments. Must be prepared, signed and sealed by duly licensed Civil Engineers, Sanitary Engineers or Environmental Planners.
2. **Traffic Impact Statement** — required of major, high intensity facilities such as commercial-residential buildings or condominiums **having four floors and above**, shopping malls, public markets, transportation terminals/garages, schools, universities, residential and industrial subdivisions, cock fighting arena, sports stadia and other similar developments; plus any other traffic generating development as determined by the Zoning Administrator/Zoning Officer.

## Section 7. Geological Hazard per soil types

## Section 8. Miscellaneous Provisions

- **NOISE AND VIBRATIONS** — All noise and vibration-producing machinery shall be enclosed by a building, provided with effective noise-absorbing materials, noise silencers and mufflers, and **an open yard of a distance of not less than three metres (3 m.) from the street or adjoining property lines**, with property planted to dense trees as buffers. Machinery should be mounted on shock-absorbing mountings (cork set on reinforced concrete foundations, or floating isolated foundation set on piles). "Noise which objectionable due to intermittence beat frequency or high pitch shall not be allowed unless enclosed in sound-proof buildings as tested and approved by the city officials concerned." **No dB limit is stated anywhere in Article VI.**
- **SMOKE** — Smoke emitted from any source **for a period aggregating seven (7) minutes in any thirty (30) minute interval**, particularly when starting a new fire, **shall have a density not greater than No. 2 of the Ringlemann Chart**.
- **DUST, DIRT AND FLY ASH** — Emission that pollutes the air or impairs visibility is not permitted. "In no case whatsoever, shall dust, dirt or fly ash be allowed to **exceed 0.3 gram per cubic meter of flue gas at stack temperature at 0° Centigrade** or to create a haze with **opaqueness equivalent to or greater than No. 1 of the Ringlemann Chart**."
- **ODORS AND GASES** — Emission of foul odors and gases deleterious to public health, safety and general welfare not permitted. Such activities shall be enclosed by airtight buildings provided with air-conditioning system filters, deodorizing and other air-cleaning equipment.
- **GLARE AND HEAT** — Glare and heat from operation or activity shall not be radiated, seen or felt from any point beyond the limits of the property.
- **INDUSTRIAL WASTE** — Industrial plant wastes disposed of only in a manner which will not create nuisance or danger to adjoining properties or the community.
- **SEWERAGE DISPOSAL** — No sewerage dangerous to public health, safety and general welfare discharged into any public sewer system, natural waterway or drainage channel; all sewerage shall comply with EMB/DENR requirements. **Acidity or alkalinity neutralized to a pH between 6.5 and 8.5 as a daily average on a volumetric basis, with a temporary variation of pH 5.0 to pH 10.0. Waste water shall not contain grease and oil in excess of 300 PPM or exceed a daily average of 10 PPM.**
- **PETITION TO HOMEOWNER'S ASSOCIATION OF BARANGAY** — Where a person plans to establish a use/activity that will necessarily affect the character of a residential zone in terms of traffic generated and/or opening the area to outsiders resulting in loss of privacy of residents, **the prior approval of a majority of the household heads of the homeowner's association, or in its absence the barangay, most especially the persons immediately adjacent to the proposed site, must be secured as one of the pre-requisites for locational clearance and building permit.**
- **PROVISIONS FOR ROAD WIDENING** — Reclassification from Residential to Commercial along Gov. W. Pascual Avenue (block deep) and Gen. P. Borromeo Street (lot deep) would generate heavy vehicle volume, requiring road widening provision. A proponent applying for a locational clearance on a specific project activity **requires a reasonable setback of three (3) metres on both sides of Gov. W. Pascual Avenue**; and **Sanciangco St., Gen. Borromeo Street, Don Basilio Bautista Blvd., J.P. Rizal Avenue, M.H. Del Pilar St., and Panghulo Road require a setback of one (1) metre on both sides**, in accordance with the National Building Code.
  (As printed, the sentence lists Gov. W. Pascual Avenue in both clauses; the 3 m rule reads onto Gov. W. Pascual Ave. and the 1 m rule onto the six named streets.)

# Part 3 — General Regulations (Article VII) and Mitigating Devices (Article VIII)

## Article VII — General Regulations

### Section 1. Height Regulations
1. Where an **R-1 district adjoins a C-2 district** without an intervening street or permanent open space **over 6 metres** wide, and where zoning lots separated by district boundaries have adjacent front yards, the proposed structure fronting the R-1 district shall be limited to **12 metres or four storeys, whichever is lesser**.
2. Where an **R-1 or R-2 district adjoins a C-2 or C-3** without an intervening street or permanent open space **over 4 metres** wide, the proposed C-2/C-3 structure adjacent to the R-1 zone shall be limited to **9 metres or three storeys, whichever is lesser**.
3. Where an **R-1, R-2 or R-3 district adjoins an I-1 or I-2 zone** without an intervening street or permanent open space **over 4 metres** wide, the proposed industrial structure fronting the residential zone shall be limited to **9 metres or three storeys, whichever is lower**.
4. Structures within the zones below shall not exceed the height requirements measured from the finished ground floor line to the highest point of a flat roof; or to the average height of the highest gable or hip roof; or the top of a parapet wall. On sloping ground the average ground level of a buildable area is the established grade elevation.

### Section 2. Area Regulations
Area regulations in all zones shall conform to the applicable minimum requirements of existing laws, codes and regulations, listed as: PD 957; BP 220; RA 7279 (UDHA); PD 1096 (National Building Code); PD 1185 (Fire Code); PD 856 (Sanitation Code); RA 6541 (Structural Code); BP 344 (Accessibility Law); HLURB Town Planning and Zoning Program rules; CA 141 (Public Land Act — public lands including foreshore and reclaimed); PD 705 (Revised Forestry Code — forestlands); PD 1076 [sic] Water Code — inland and coastal waters, shorelines and riverbank easements; RA 6657 (CARL — agrarian reform lands); RA 7279 (UDHA — socialized housing and settlements); RA 7586 (NIPAS — protected areas land and sea); RA 7942 (Philippine Mining Act — mining areas); RA 8371 (IPRA — ancestral lands); RA 8435 (AFMA — SAFDZs and prime agricultural lands); RA 8550 (Revised Fisheries Code — municipal waters and coastal zones); RA 9593 (Philippine Tourism Act — tourism zones and estates); RA 9729 (Climate Change Act, as amended); RA 10066 (Cultural Heritage Act — cultural and heritage zones/areas); RA 100121 [sic] (Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act — disaster-prone and geo-hazard areas); and other relevant guidelines promulgated by the national agencies concerned.
**No numeric minimum lot areas are stated in Article VII — all are delegated by reference.**

### Section 3. Yard, off-Street Parking Space
No part of a road, off-street parking space, loading space, or other required open space of any building may, for purposes of complying with this Ordinance, be counted as part of the yard, open space, off-street parking or loading space similarly required of any adjacent neighbouring building. Exception: buildings with **common party walls or adjacent building lines**, in which case only the yard for the free or non-abutting sides is required.

### Section 4. Traffic Generators
All traffic generating buildings and structures allowed in any district **must provide a minimum setback of five (5) metres for parking spaces** for their employees, clients and visitors.

### Section 5. Advertising and Business Signs
Advertising and business signs displayed or put up for public view in any district must comply with existing laws, rules and regulations. (One sentence; the operative rule is Section 13.)

### Section 6. Erection of more than one principal structure
Where more than one structure may be permitted on a single lot, the yard and other requirements of the Ordinance shall be met **for each structure as though it were to be erected on an individual lot**.

### Section 7. Dwellings on rear lots
No building used or designed for use as a residence shall be allowed on any rear lot unless the lot has a **right-of-way easement over a path of at least four (4) metres** leading to a street. Two or more buildings may be allowed on a common path if the **right-of-way easement is at least six (6) metres wide**.

### Section 8. Structure to have access
Every building erected or moved shall have access to a public street, or to a private street open to the public, and all structures shall be so located on lots as to provide safe and convenient access for reserving fire protection units.

### Section 9. Yard requirements along a district boundary line
Lots abutting a district boundary line shall conform to the yard requirements of the **more restrictive** district bounded by the line.

### Section 10. Dwelling group
Where it is impractical to apply the zoning regulations to an individual building unit in a residential compound of two or more buildings, a permit for construction of the compound may be issued provided the plan conforms to:
- buildings used only for residential purposes and such uses permitted in the district;
- average lot area per family of dwelling units in the compound, exclusive of area used for streets or driveways, **not less than the lot area per family required in the district**;
- an open space for playground purposes within the tract of **at least an aggregate 5% of the required lot area per family, but in no case less than 100 square metres**; provided that where the compound is intended for **fewer than ten families** the playground may be dispensed with; and provided further that such open space may count as part of the yard requirements for the compound;
- an adequate private garage or off-street parking area within or immediately adjacent to the tract, depending on the needs of residents and visitors.

### Section 11. Buffer Regulations
> "A buffer shall be define as an additional open space different from the building setback. A buffer of four (4) meters shall be provided along entire boundary length between two or more conflicting zones to mitigate adverse impacts and nuisance between two adjacent developments aside from providing light and ventilation."
Regulations:
- **The more intense development/use shall provide the four (4) metre buffer.**
- The buffer strip should be open and not encroached upon by any building or structure; it should be part of the yard or open space and may be used as a planting strip.
- **No build zone.**
- The buffer may be **extended beyond four (4) metres** whenever necessary to ameliorate negative conditions such as, but not limited to, noise, odor, unsightly building, or danger from fires and explosion.

### Section 12. Specific provisions in the National Building Code
Specific provisions of PD 1096 as amended, relevant to traffic generators, advertising and business signs, erection of more than one principal structure, dwelling on rear lots, access yard requirements and dwelling groups, which are not in conflict with the ZO, shall be observed.

### Section 13. Advertising, Billboards and Business Signs
**No advertising, billboard or business sign, whether on or off the premises of an establishment, may be displayed or put up for public view without locational clearance from the Zoning Administrator/Zoning Officer.** Locational clearance may be granted only when the sign is appropriate for the permitted use of the zone and the size is not excessive, taking into account the bulk or size of the building and the business practices or usages of the locality, and it shall in no case obstruct the view of any scenic spot.
Obnoxious signs constituting a nuisance to adjoining property owners, distracting motorists, or constituting hazards to public safety are not allowed in any area. No sign should project onto public property unless expressly allowed by the Zoning Administrator/Zoning Officer. **Temporary signs and billboards for not more than two months** may be allowed by the Zoning Officer/Administrator upon payment of corresponding fees; the permit shall indicate location, size, slope, contents and type of construction.
1. No advertising billboards and business signs shall be installed so as to unduly obstruct the natural view of landscape and heritage structures or otherwise defile, debase or offend aesthetic and cultural values and traditions.
2. Commercial signs of all forms intruding into Road Right of Way (RROW) leading to or away from important public or historical/cultural buildings/structures shall not be allowed, especially commercial signs supported from any building projection (such as arcades) or similar structure.
3. View corridors or lines of sight from historical/heritage buildings/structures shall not be entirely blocked by the height of intervening structures. Maximum height of intervening properties shall be determined so as not to obstruct the line of sight.
**It shall be unlawful to maintain an obsolete sign by reason of discontinuance of business, service or activity for more than 60 days therefrom.**

## Article VIII — Mitigating Devices

### Section 1 (captioned "Infrastructure Capacities" — see anomalies; contents are the substantive grounds for Variances and Exceptions)
> "Variances and/or Exceptions from the provisions of this Ordinance may be allowed by the Local Zoning Board of Appeals (LZBA) only when the following terms and conditions exist:"

**1. Variances** (deviation from applicable Building Bulk and Density Regulations, Building Design Regulations and Performance Standards). "Variance may be allowed provided that proposals satisfy **all** of the following provisions:"
- a. Conforming to the provisions of the Ordinance will cause **undue hardship** on the part of the owner of the property due to **physical conditions of the property (topography, shape, etc.), which is not self-created**.
- b. The proposed variance is the **minimum deviation necessary** to permit reasonable use of the property.
- c. The variance **will not alter the intended physical character of the zone** and adversely affect the use of other properties in the same zone, such as blocking off natural light, causing loss of natural ventilation, or encroaching in public easements and the like.
- d. The variance **will not weaken the general purpose of the Ordinance** and will not adversely affect the public health, safety or welfare.
- e. The variance **will be in harmony with the spirit of this Ordinance**.

**2. Exceptions** (deviations from Allowable Use provisions). "Exceptions may be allowed provided that proposals satisfy **all** of the following conditions:"
- a. The exception **will not adversely affect the public health, safety and welfare** and is in keeping with the general pattern of development in the community.
- b. The proposed project shall **support economic based activities/provide livelihood, vital community services and facilities** while at the same time posing no adverse effect on the zone/community.
- c. The exception **will not adversely affect the appropriate use of adjoining properties in the same zone**, such as generating excessive vehicular traffic, causing overcrowding of people, or generating excessive noise and the like.
- d. The exception **will not alter the essential character and general purpose of the zone** where the exception sought is located.

### Section 2. Procedures for Evaluating Variances and/or Exceptions
Full procedure as printed:
1. The **project proponent files a written application** for Variance and/or Exception **with the LZBA**, citing the section(s) of the Ordinance under which the same is sought and stating the ground/s thereof.
2. Upon filing, **a visible project sign** (indicating the name and nature of the proposed project) shall be posted at the project site, **maintained until the LZBA has rendered a decision**.
3. The **LZBA shall conduct preliminary studies** on the application. **The application papers shall be made accessible to the public.**
4. A **written affidavit of no objection** to the project **by the owners of the properties immediately in front of and abutting the project site** shall be filed by the applicant with the LZBA **within fifteen (15) days upon filing of application**.
5. The **LZBA shall hold public hearing(s) to be held in the concerned barangay**.
6. At the hearing, any party may appear in person or be represented by agent/s.
7. All interested parties shall be accorded the opportunity to be heard and present evidences and testimonies.
8. The **LZBA shall render a decision within thirty (30) days from the filing of the application**, exclusive of the time spent for preparation of the written affidavit of non-objection and the public hearing(s).
9. **All expenses incurred in evaluating proposals for Variances and/or Exceptions shall be shouldered by the project proponent.**

**Decision-maker:** the Local Zoning Board of Appeals (LZBA) throughout — it receives the application, conducts preliminary studies, holds the hearing, and renders the decision. The Zoning Administrator/Zoning Officer has no role stated in Article VIII. (Appeals from LZBA decisions and the LZBA's composition are Article IX Sections 17–18, outside this digest.)

# Anomalies

## Terms used but never defined in Article III
- **Buffer** — regulated (Art. VII Sec. 11; Art. VI Sec. 5 item 6; Art. VI Sec. 8 NOISE) and used inside other definitions, but not in the Art. III list. Only a mid-text definition in Art. VII Sec. 11.
- **Setback** — not a defined term; only reachable through the **Yard** definition ("The width of the yard is the setback"). Numeric setbacks are imposed in Art. VI Sec. 8 and Art. VII Sec. 4.
- **Institutional Zone (IZ)** — a base zone (Art. V Sec. 2.20, and a row in the Art. VII height table), but Article III defines only **Special Institutional Zone (SI-Z)**.
- **Easement Zone (EZ)** — a base zone (Art. V Sec. 2.14) but only **Easement** (the open space) is defined.
- **Fishpond Zone** — a base zone (Art. V Sec. 2.16) but only **Fish Pond** (the facility) is defined.
- **Municipal Waters Zone** — referenced by four sub-zone definitions (Aq-SZ, CF-SZ, Mn-Z) but never itself defined.
- **Forest Zone** — referenced by the Civil Reservation Sub-Zone definition but never defined.
- **Sub-Zone** — the generic concept is used throughout (Art. IV Sec. 1 "Division into Zones or Sub-Zones") but has no definition, unlike **Base Zones** and **Overlay Zones**.
- **Building Bulk and Density Regulations** / **Building Design Regulations** — the two categories a Variance operates on (Art. III "Variance", Art. VIII Sec. 1) are never defined as terms.
- No Art. III term ties a **business** (as opposed to a building or a parcel) to a zoning requirement; the business trigger exists only in Art. IX Sec. 8.

## Terms defined but with no corresponding regulation section
- **Billboards Overlay Zone (BB-OZ)**, **Key Biodiversity Area Overlay Zone (KBA-OZ)**, and **Urban Corridor Overlay Zone (UCD-OZ)** are defined in Article III, but Article V Section 4 (Regulations in Overlay Zones) contains only 4.1 Flood, 4.2 Ecotourism, 4.3 Heritage.
