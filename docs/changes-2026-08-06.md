# 1. The two critical ones

## Filings could never issue permits

Approving one office's review moved a filing to *For Inspection*. The review sheet then keyed on the
**filing's** status alone — so the five offices that still owed a review lost their Approve button
entirely. No approval, no inspection, no permits. Permanently. `BIZ-2026-00958` was in that state.

**Fixed:** the sheet keys on **this office's own assignment**. An office that still owes a review gets
its sheet; one that has finished gets only the compact box. Both on the same filing at once.

That is also why this *honours* your earlier instruction rather than reverting it — you asked twice
for the form to go, and both times you were looking at a seat whose review was already done.

## Terminal filings could be revived

Any office's approval dragged a `returned`, `rejected` or `cancelled` filing back into *For
Inspection*. Proven on a copy of the register, not argued: `rejected → for_inspection`, history row
written, inspection booked. **101 rejected filings carried that exposure.**

**Fixed:** a transition legality table, guarded at four depths. A returned filing keeps the
applicant's revision request instead of silently losing it.

# 4. What changed, by area

## Workflow

- Each office's approval books **its own** inspection immediately, rather than all six waiting for the
  slowest office to finish its paperwork.
- Permits issue only when **every review is complete and every current visit has passed** — both
  conditions, one predicate, checked at both call sites.
- Terminal filings cannot be revived. Illegal transitions are refused loudly, not silently.

## Separability

- An office receives **only its own office form**. The shared sheet survives untouched — address,
  barangay, PSIC line, uploads, floor area — because CENRO reviews the PSIC line, CPDO's fee is per
  square metre, and an inspector who cannot see the address cannot find the premises.
- The sheet now leads with the reading office's own clearance and names that office in the header.
- The `admin` bypass in department checks is gone, replaced by a structural rule rather than a role
  name. Super-admin OIC reassignment still works, keyed on what the account *is*, not what it is called.

## Location insights

- **Every division now names what it makes** — `Rubber & Plastics`, `Concrete, Glass & Ceramics`,
  `Furniture Manufacturing`. Your six neighbours read as six trades instead of one word six times.
- Dairy resolves to **Food & Beverage Manufacturing**, no longer swallowed by a bucket that excluded it.
- A third figure reconciles the two rows: *"none of your trade, 2 in your category, plastics is the
  block's most common"* — no PSIC hierarchy reasoning required of the applicant.
- The defect's *shape* is now a test: **no label may contain another**. Checked across all 135 codes.

## Analytics copy

- Every window the four selectors offer is precomputed. Measured before committing: **8 → 19
  snapshots, 2.4 MB → 6.6 MB** nightly.
- The only notice that still appears is the one where the Refresh button beside it is the actual fix.

## Clearances

- Two routes out of an applied clearance, because one is not enough: a `Withdraw` link, and Submit
  offering to switch. The second cannot free a mistaken Market application — there is no certificate
  to upload — which is why both exist.
- Apply over an existing upload now shows a red dialog naming the file: **"Delete & apply"** or
  **"Keep my copy"**.
- All five unsubmittable drafts verified free — driven through the real endpoints on a copy of the
  register, two taken all the way to `pending_payment`. **Your live data was not touched.**
