# The renewal process, as decided 17 September 2026

Written before the code, so the decisions can be checked against what the LGU
said rather than reverse-engineered from a diff later. Every heading below is a
client decision from that conversation; the notes under each are the
consequences I could find in the code.

Supersedes the renewal half of `application-flow-2026-09.md`. The NEW-filing
flow in that document is unchanged.

---

## 1. Each renewal is independent

A renewal filing carries exactly the permits the applicant ticked — this is
already true (`WorkflowService::attachRequiredPermitTypes` syncs the ticked
prior permits rather than expanding to all five). What changes is that the
permits no longer wait for each other, and the business permit no longer waits
for them.

## 2. The business permit expires on 20 January, whatever the issue date

New and renewal alike. A permit issued in June 2026 expires 20 January 2027 — a
seven-month first term; one issued in December 2026 expires 20 January 2027, a
one-month term.

**20 January is not invented here.** It is already in the codebase twice, from
the ordinance:

- `ApplicationController.php` — "Ordinance Sec. 2N: annual (first 20 days of
  January) or quarterly"
- `ChatbotResponder.php` — "Business permits are renewed during the first 20
  days of January every year", which the chatbot already tells applicants.

The renewal window is 1–20 January, so a permit expiring on the 20th means
renewing inside the window keeps cover continuous.

**This reverses a decision of 9 September 2026**, recorded in
`WorkflowService::issuePermitFor`: the calendar-year convention was put to the
client then and *not* chosen, in favour of "a renewal continues the term". It is
reversed for the BUSINESS permit only. The other five keep continue-the-term,
which is what makes renewing them early not a penalty.

## 3. The other five permits: 1 year, renewable any time

Unchanged. `validity_days = 365` on all of them, and `issuePermitFor` continues
an unexpired term rather than restarting it — so a sanitary permit renewed in
April, with the old one running to June, is valid June→June.

## 4. A business-permit renewal has no Awaiting Other Permits

    For Initial Approval → Pending Payment → For Final Approval → Approved

Four stages. Both of BPLO's acts survive; only the gathering stage goes, because
there is nothing to gather: the applicant already holds the clearances and
uploads them.

Needs `ApplicationStatus::allowedNext` to admit
`PendingPayment → ForFinalApproval`, and `refreshReadiness` not to pull the
filing back out.

## 5. Uploaded clearance copies do not go to the offices

On a business-permit renewal the applicant uploads certificates those offices
have **already issued**. So:

- no assignment is raised to CHO / BFP / OBO / CENRO / CPDO
- no office form to fill in
- no inspection
- the pivot row counts as **satisfied by the upload**, so `approveOverall`'s
  "no required permit outstanding" test passes

BPLO reads the copies at Final Approval. If one is expired or missing, BPLO is
**warned, not blocked** — and its way of asking for a better copy is the Return
feature built on 17 September, pointer included. Nothing here gates issuance.

## 6. A clearance renewed on its own never reaches BPLO

    For Initial Approval → (For Inspection) → Approved

The issuing office throughout. No Pending Payment, no Final Approval, no BPLO.
"For Initial Approval" on such a filing means *that office* is reading the form,
not BPLO — which is a change to what the first stage means and is the part of
this most likely to surprise a reader.

## 7. Payment happens only at the January business-permit renewal

A clearance renewed in June is approved and **issued unpaid**. Its fee becomes a
receivable against the BUSINESS, and the next business-permit renewal's Tax
Order of Payment sweeps in every unbilled fee.

    Jun 2026  Sanitary renewal approved → permit issued
              ₱1,200 recorded unbilled, on the business
    Aug 2026  FSIC renewal approved → permit issued
              ₱900 unbilled
    Jan 2027  Business permit renewal
              TOP = business permit fees + ₱1,200 + ₱900

This is the largest change in the list, because nothing in the system can
currently issue a permit that has not been paid for: the bill gates the
clearance stage (`ClearanceService::isUnlocked` asks `status->isPaid()`) and
`approveOverall` mints certificates only on a settled filing.

## 8. Unbilled fees wait indefinitely, and are visible

No automatic penalty, no lapsing permit. The receivable stands until a business
permit renewal collects it, and it shows on the business record and in the
admin's arrears view so nobody has to remember it.

**Deliberately not built:** surcharge or interest on a deferred fee. Whether
Sec. 2N's surcharge applies to a fee the LGU itself chose to defer is BPLO's
reading to give, not ours to assume.

## Built, as of 18 September 2026

All seven decisions are implemented. Two more were taken on 18 September when
§6 turned out to need them:

- **A clearance-only renewal closes itself** the moment its last permit is
  issued — no further press by anyone. It needed a `For Initial Approval →
  Approved` edge and `refreshReadiness` to watch that status for a filing where
  `defersPayment()` is true.
- **Unbilled fees surface on the business record**: a total and an itemised
  breakdown on the admin roster, with `on_a_bill` marking a fee already sitting
  on a renewal awaiting payment.

Two live bugs were found while building §6 and are fixed:

- `approveMainForm` guarded only on STATUS, so BPLO could approve — and
  therefore bill — a clearance-only renewal that must defer. A money bug.
- `refreshReadiness` could never close such a filing, so its permit was issued
  and the filing sat at For Initial Approval for ever, claiming an office was
  reading something it had already granted.

Tests: `RenewalSkipsGatheringTest`, `DeferredPermitFeeTest`,
`UnbilledFeeVisibilityTest`, plus `PartialRenewalTest` re-aimed at the new flow
and `WorkflowHappyPathTest` re-aimed at the 20 January rule.

## 9. The renewal form carries last year's answers forward

Decided 18 September 2026. A renewal's office form is the SAME form as a new
application's — the office wants the same facts about the premises every year —
so last year's answers are **offered**, filled in and **flagged per field**, and
the applicant confirms rather than re-types.

Offered, never applied: `prefill` is its own key on the office-forms payload,
not merged into `form_data`. The sheet seeds empty fields from it and the
autosave baseline is the merged object, so a carried answer nobody has touched
does not look like an edit — it reaches the register only when the applicant
saves or submits. Folded into `form_data` it would be indistinguishable from a
reviewed answer on reload, and would autosave last year's words into the
register as this year's, on a form the applicant signs.

**Three kinds of answer never carry** (`App\Support\RenewalPrefill`):

- **Derived** — they must describe THIS filing. Excluded by asking
  `OfficeFormAnswers::derive` what it produces from an empty payload, so the
  list maintains itself.
- **Officer-written** — `building_permit_date`, `fsec_date`, `date_issued`.
  Carrying them would print last year's issuance dates on this year's
  certificate.
- **Attestations** — `certified`. A signature is an act, not a fact. This is the
  one that would be genuinely wrong rather than untidy, and it also clears the
  gate that makes the applicant tick it deliberately.

No "anything changed?" short-circuit. It was offered — mirroring
MCG-BPLO-FO-002's own Section A, where a No ends the form — and the client chose
prefill alone: one mechanism, and the applicant still puts their eyes on every
answer.

## 10. A renewal's uploaded copy is not re-inspected

Confirmed 18 September 2026, resolving the tension between two earlier
decisions. On a NEW filing an uploaded copy still routes to its office and is
still inspected — 6 September, *"the LGU inspects the premises, not the
paperwork."* On a RENEWAL it does not: the office already inspected before it
issued that certificate, BPLO reads the copy, and no office is involved.

The risk the client accepted: a business whose premises deteriorated since the
last visit renews on an older certificate. The trade is that a renewal finishes
in days instead of waiting on up to five separate visits, which is the waiting
that removing Awaiting Other Permits was meant to end.

## 11. An unticked permit is an answer, not a gap

Client's correction, 18 September 2026, and it reframed the renewal picker:

> there are cases that an other permit was not needing renewal even though the
> business permit is being renewed. This is because a business permit is renewed
> every January and other permits have 1 year validity, so an other permit can
> be reused for a business permit renewal as long as this other permit is still
> valid/not expired.

So leaving a permit unticked is the **commonest** answer on a January renewal,
not an omission. The picker now says so per row — "Still valid to 12 Jun 2027 —
no renewal needed", or "Expired — tick it to renew" — rather than asking for a
click.

## 12. No permit is ever renewed on paper

Same conversation: *"There could be no cases where the permit was renewed on a
different/manual system."* This retires the year-one escape built on 30 August,
and with it two pieces of UI:

- **"None of these — my permit was issued on paper"**, the checkbox under the
  permit list. It asked for a declaration about a case that cannot happen.
- **"Not renewing one through BizTrack? Attach the paper copy."** This one was
  also wrong on its own terms: `uncoveredTypes` was built from the business's
  **own BizTrack permits**, so the panel asked for paper copies of certificates
  sitting in the register one list above it, and it appeared before the
  applicant had answered anything. Held certificates now have exactly one home,
  the Upload button in LGU Clearances, which §5 already gave them.

A renewal therefore **must** name the permit it carries forward. A business
holding none is filing a New Application and is told so, in the dialog and again
at submit.

**The column stays.** `applications.prior_permit_declared_none` holds `true` on
one filing, which is `approved` and terminal — a real answer honestly given
under the rule of the day. Nothing writes it now; dropping it would erase it.

## 13. Final Approval reads the register, not the filing

The consequence of §11 that nearly went unnoticed.
`attachRequiredPermitTypes()` attaches only the ticked permits, so a business
permit renewed alone in January carries **one** pivot row — and BPLO arrived at
the stage whose *"only purpose was to check if all clearance permits are done"*
with nothing whatever to check. The five certificates were in the register, on
last year's filings.

`App\Support\ClearanceStanding` reads them off the **business**: every required
clearance, the certificate held against it, and when it lapses. Four states, and
`missing` is deliberately not `expired` — a business that never held an FSIC and
one whose FSIC lapsed in March are different conversations.

Warned, not blocked, per §5. Gated to `readsEveryOffice` (BPLO and the super
admin): five offices' certificates in one array is a cross-office view by
construction, and this is the fifth door of that shape on `ApplicationResource`.

## A live defect found on the way, and fixed

Not renewal work, but found while chasing the one red test in the suite and
worth recording next to it. Three places asked "is this permit for inspection"
and none asked whether the filing was still **alive**, because the pivot rows
are never walked back when BPLO rejects a filing.

Measured on a rejected filing: conducting a **passing** visit answered `200`,
moved the permit to `approved`, and **issued the certificate** — `permits`
0 → 1. A Fire Safety clearance minted against a filing the LGU had refused.

One predicate now — `ApplicationPermitType::awaitingInspection()` — asked by all
three. Walking the pivot rows back in `rejectApplication()` was the alternative
and was rejected: `ClearanceStatus::Rejected` went on 17 September, so there is
no state to walk them to, and where each permit stood when the filing was
refused is worth keeping.

## Open, and flagged rather than guessed

- **No January lock.** The client was explicit: the business permit renewal
  season is January, but *do not enforce it yet*. Filing one in June is allowed
  and gets a permit expiring 20 January.
- **Surcharge on deferred fees** — see §8.
- **Which fee schedule prices a deferred clearance**: the rates at the time it
  was issued, or at the time it is billed. §7 records the amount when the permit
  is issued, which answers it as "at issue" — but it is a policy question and
  BPLO has not been asked.
